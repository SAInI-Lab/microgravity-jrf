How do human cells age when gravity stops pulling?

Every cell on Earth has evolved under a constant 1g mechanical load. Take it away and cells change their shape, their signaling, and how they age. In this ISRO collaboration you will grow human cells under simulated microgravity and find out what that does to aging. You will own the project end to end.

What you will do

  1. Culture

    Human cells in 2D and 3D under simulated microgravity, with 1g controls running alongside.

  2. Track aging

    Follow aging and senescence phenotypes over time, and design experiments that tell real effects from artefacts.

  3. Measure

    Molecular readouts such as qPCR, western blots, immunofluorescence and imaging, then whatever the biology asks for next.

  4. Own

    Design, troubleshooting, analysis and writing. When something is stuck, you are the one who gets it moving.

Skills we are looking for

Must have

  • A master’s degree in life sciences, biotechnology, bioengineering or a related field
  • Hands-on mammalian cell culture and sterile technique
  • At least one of qPCR, western blotting or immunofluorescence
  • A careful notebook: records someone else could repeat
  • Basic data analysis in Excel, R or Python

Good to have

  • Senescence assays (SA-β-gal, p16/p21, SASP)
  • 3D culture or spheroids
  • Microscopy and image analysis (Fiji/ImageJ)
  • A habit of fixing and tinkering with instruments
  • A qualified fellowship exam such as CSIR/UGC NET, GATE or DBT-BET
